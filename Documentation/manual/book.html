About This Manual

MacPhoto Utility turns your photographs into pictures for a Macintosh Plus: from each one, a print image, the size of a playing card in 256 greys, for MacPhoto on the Plus to edit and for printing; and a screen image, the Plus’s whole screen in black and white. This manual shows you how.

Two computers

MacPhoto Utility runs on a modern Mac: this manual calls it “your Mac.” The pictures it makes are for the Macintosh Plus (“the Plus”), and travel there on a disk made by its companion, the Macintosh Transfer Utility.

What you need

  • an Apple silicon Mac with macOS 26;
  • your photographs: JPEG, HEIC, PNG or TIFF;
  • the Macintosh Transfer Utility, to carry the files to the Plus;
  • a Macintosh Plus with System 6.0.7 and a BlueSCSI.

What you should know

This manual assumes you know your way round your Mac and the Finder. For the Plus, the Macintosh Owner’s Guide covers the basics.

How to use this manual

  • Learn it: Chapter 1, “Learning MacPhoto Utility,” takes one photograph from your Mac to the Plus, in six short lessons.
  • Look it up: Chapter 2, “Using MacPhoto Utility,” shows how to do each task. Chapter 3 is the reference. The appendixes list the messages, the files on the Plus, what to do when something goes wrong, and the specifications.
  • Just start: add a photograph and try it.

Important

MacPhoto Utility never changes your photographs. But if you choose to replace files when exporting, the old ones are gone.

What MacPhoto Utility Does

A photograph on your Mac holds millions of colours. The Macintosh Plus shows two: black and white, on a screen 512 dots wide and 342 high. MacPhoto Utility bridges the gap. From each photograph it makes two pictures:

  • a print image, the size of a playing card (2.5 by 3.5 inches) in 256 shades of grey, for MacPhoto on the Plus to edit, and for printing;
  • a screen image, the Plus’s whole screen in black and white, to look at on the Plus as it is, or to show in After Dark’s slide show.

Your photograph itself is never changed. MacPhoto Utility only reads it.

Two frames

The print and the screen are different shapes. A print standing upright is tall and narrow; the screen is wide. No single frame suits both, so each photograph has two: a print crop and a screen crop, which you set separately.

From your Mac to the Plus

MacPhoto Utility works on your Mac, a modern one. The files it makes travel to the Plus on a disk made by the Macintosh Transfer Utility, its companion:

1Your photographJPEG, HEIC, PNG or TIFF, on your Mac
2MacPhoto Utilityframed twice: a print image and a screen image
3Macintosh Transfer Utilityputs the files on a disk for the Plus
4The Macintosh PlusMacPhoto, SuperPaint, MacPaint, After Dark

What you’ll learn

The lessons that follow take about a quarter of an hour, with one of your own photographs. You’ll install MacPhoto Utility, add a photograph, frame it twice, export it, and see it on the Plus.

You should already know your way round your Mac and the Finder. For the Plus, the Macintosh Owner’s Guide covers the basics.

1Installing MacPhoto Utility

MacPhoto Utility comes as a single file, MacPhoto-Utility-B-0.9.0.zip (or the version you have).

Double-click the zip file.

The Finder unpacks it beside the zip: the MacPhoto Utility program.

Drag MacPhoto Utility to the Applications folder, in the sidebar of a Finder window.

Open the Applications folder, and double-click MacPhoto Utility.

Its window opens, empty for now. It needs an Apple silicon Mac with macOS 26.

If macOS won’t open it the first time, open System Settings, click Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway beside its name. It’s signed for its owner’s own use, not by Apple.

2Adding a Photograph

Choose Add Photographs from the File menu (or press Command-I), select a photograph, and click Open.

OR

Drag a photograph from the Finder onto the window.

MacPhoto Utility reads JPEG, HEIC, PNG and TIFF photographs: the kinds a phone or a camera makes. The photograph appears in the list on the left, with a small picture of it.

Click the photograph in the list.

It opens in the middle of the window, the right way up whichever way the camera was held. Underneath are two previews: the print, and the Plus’s screen.

The panel at the bottom left gives the photograph’s details: what kind of file it is, its size, and whether it was turned to stand upright.

3Framing the Print

The print is 2.5 by 3.5 inches, so it can’t always show the whole photograph. You choose the part it shows, and everything else is left out of the print, but never out of your photograph.

Click Print Crop, above the picture, if it isn’t already chosen.

A white frame shows what will be printed. Everything outside it is dimmed. The frame keeps the print’s shape however you change it.

Drag inside the frame to move it.

The print preview, below the picture, follows a moment later. It’s the print itself, made from the whole photograph, not a sketch.

Drag the zoom slider to the right, or drag one of the frame’s corners towards the middle.

The frame gets smaller, so the print shows less of the photograph, larger. To start again, click Reset.

A frame that’s too small for the print gets a warning: see “Small Photographs” in Chapter 2.

Click Portrait or Landscape to turn the print the other way.

The frame changes shape, and keeps its place on the picture.

4Framing the Screen Image

Click Screen Crop (or press the Tab key).

Now the frame is the Plus’s screen: wide, and always filled edge to edge. The print’s frame stays, as a thin dashed line, so you can see both.

Move and size the frame as you did the print’s.

The Mac Plus screen preview shows the screen image exactly as it will look on the Plus: one dot of the Plus’s screen for each dot here, in black and white.

Shades of grey become patterns of black and white dots, in the way Bill Atkinson devised for the first Macintosh. If a photograph looks too dark or too flat, drag Brightness or Contrast under the preview; they change the screen image, never the print. Normal puts them back.

Click Larger to see the screen image twice the size.

Click Done to put it away.

5Exporting

The files go into a folder you choose, on your Mac.

Under Output, click Choose, select a folder (or make a new one), and click Choose.

MacPhoto Utility remembers it for next time. Leave the other settings as they are: a print at 360 dots an inch, and two files, the print PICT and the screen PICT.

Click Export This Photograph (or press Command-E).

MacPhoto Utility makes the two files, checks each one by reading it back, and lists them: the print image in a folder called Print Files, the screen image in PICT Files.

Click Show in Finder to see them.

They’re ready for the Plus, but not on it yet.

6Seeing It on the Macintosh Plus

The Macintosh Transfer Utility puts the files on a disk for the Plus, in folders of the same names, with each file marked as a picture for the Plus to recognise. CHECK

On the Plus, open the PICT Files folder, then open the screen image in SuperPaint (or another program that opens PICT files).

Your photograph fills the Plus’s screen, just as the preview showed it.

This picture, and the MacPaint one below, are from an emulated Macintosh Plus. CHECK

To use the screen image in After Dark’s slide show, copy it into the Slide Show Folder in the After Dark Files folder, in the System Folder. CHECK

Only screen images belong there. The print images are in 256 greys, which the Plus’s own drawing can’t show.

The print image waits in Print Files for MacPhoto, the Plus’s photograph editor. Photoshop 1.0 can open it too. CHECK

If you also export a MacPaint document, MacPaint shows the screen image in the middle of its page. Your patterns are left as they were.

What Now?

That’s the whole journey: add, frame twice, export, and carry the files to the Plus.

Chapter 2, “Using MacPhoto Utility,” covers each task in turn: a folder of photographs at once, the queue, the screen image’s brightness, small photographs, and saving your work as a project to come back to. Chapter 3 describes every part of the window and every menu command.

Adding Photographs

MacPhoto Utility works on a list of photographs, the queue. Photographs stay where they are on your Mac; the queue only lists them.

To Add Photographs

Choose Add Photographs from the File menu (or press Command-I), select one or more photographs, and click Open.

OR

Click the + button above the list.

JPEG, HEIC, PNG and TIFF photographs are added, in the order the Finder shows them. A photograph already in the queue isn’t added twice.

To Add a Folder of Photographs

Choose Add Folder from the File menu (or press Shift-Command-I), select the folder, and click Add.

The photographs directly inside it are added; folders inside it are left alone.

To Add by Dragging

Drag photographs, or a folder, from the Finder onto the window, or onto MacPhoto Utility’s icon in the Dock.

If Some Files Are Skipped

Anything that isn’t a photograph MacPhoto Utility reads is left out, and the foot of the list says how many.

Click “skipped: not photographs” to see which, and why.

If a Photograph Can’t Be Read

A photograph that’s damaged, or too large (more than 250 megapixels), stays in the list with a red ✕. Click it to see why.

Try another copy of the photograph: from your Photos library, say, exported again.

The Queue

Each photograph in the list shows a small picture of itself, its name, how its print will stand, and how far you’ve got with it.

What the Marks Mean

Beside each name, on the right:

  • P or L: its print is portrait or landscape.
  • ○ Not looked at yet: it hasn’t been opened, so its frames are as they started, in the middle at full size.
  • ⚠ Small for its output: see “Small Photographs.”
  • ✕ Can’t be read: damaged, or not a photograph.
  • ✓ Exported: exported since it was last changed.
  • Missing: a project’s photograph that isn’t where it was (see “Projects”).

To Work on a Photograph

Click it in the list, or move to it with the Up and Down Arrow keys.

It opens with its frames just as you left them. Every photograph keeps its own.

To Take Photographs Out of the Queue

Select them, and click Remove (or press Delete).

Remove All empties the queue. Either way, the photographs themselves stay on your Mac: nothing is deleted.

Framing

Each photograph has two frames: the print’s and the screen’s. Print Crop and Screen Crop, above the picture, choose which you’re setting; the other shows as a dashed line.

To Move a Frame

Drag inside it.

It stops at the edges of the photograph: a frame never goes beyond it. The arrow keys move it a little at a time.

To Make a Frame Smaller or Larger

Drag one of its corners.

OR

Drag the zoom slider; or pinch on a trackpad, or turn the scroll wheel, over the picture.

The frame keeps its shape. It can be as large as the photograph allows, and as small as an eighth of that.

To Turn a Photograph

Click a turn button, above the picture (or press Command-[ or Command-]).

It turns a quarter at a time, frames and all.

To Turn the Print

With Print Crop chosen, click Portrait or Landscape.

A new photograph starts as landscape if it’s wider than it is tall, and as portrait otherwise.

To Start Again

Click Reset (or press Command-0).

The frame you’re setting goes back to the middle, at full size. The other is left as it is.

The Screen Image

The screen image is the Plus’s whole screen, 512 dots by 342, in black and white. The Mac Plus screen preview shows it dot for dot.

To Make It Lighter, Darker or Crisper

Drag Brightness or Contrast, under the preview.

Each goes from −100 to +100. They change the screen image only, never the print, and each photograph keeps its own. Normal puts both back to 0.

Greys become patterns of black and white dots, in the way Bill Atkinson devised for the first Macintosh. It keeps edges crisp but can lose detail in the lightest and darkest parts; a little more contrast or brightness often helps.

To See It Larger

Click Larger, above the preview.

It’s shown twice the size. Click Done to put it away.

Small Photographs

A frame needs enough of the photograph to fill its picture. When it hasn’t, MacPhoto Utility still makes the picture, enlarged, but says so.

If a Photograph Is Small for Its Output

A warning appears under the preview it affects, and the photograph is marked ⚠ in the list.

A print at 360 dots an inch needs a frame of at least 900 by 1260 of the photograph’s own pixels (750 by 1050 at 300). The screen image needs 512 by 342.

Make the frame larger, or choose 300 PPI for the print.

A photograph from any recent phone is large enough unless you zoom in a long way.

The Output

The settings under Output are shared by every photograph in the queue, and remembered.

To Choose the Print’s Resolution

Click 360 PPI or 300 PPI.

360 dots an inch gives more detail; the framing is the same either way.

To Choose Which Files Are Made

Turn on Print PICT, Screen PICT or MacPaint.

The first two are on to start with. A MacPaint document holds the screen image too, in the middle of a MacPaint page.

To Choose the Folder

Click Choose, select a folder (or make a new one), and click Choose.

The files go into folders inside it, named as they’ll be on the Plus: Print Files, PICT Files and MacPaint Files.

Exporting

Exporting makes the files. Each one is read back and checked after it’s written; a file that fails isn’t kept.

To Export One Photograph

Click Export This Photograph (or press Command-E).

The files made are listed under the button. Click Show in Finder to see them.

To Export Every Photograph

Click Export All (or press Shift-Command-E).

The photographs are exported one at a time, in the order of the list. The panel shows which one it’s on. A photograph that fails is marked, and the rest carry on.

If Some Haven’t Been Looked At

Export All asks first, and lists them: they’d be exported with the frames they started with, in the middle at full size.

Click Export All to go ahead, or Cancel to look at them first.

If Files Are Already There

When the folder already has files of the same names, MacPhoto Utility asks once, for all of them:

  • Keep Both saves the new files under the next free name: IMG_8691 2.pict, say.
  • Replace puts the new files in place of the old ones.
  • Skip Those Photographs leaves them out.
  • Cancel exports nothing.

Two photographs in the queue with the same name (from different folders) are numbered automatically.

To Stop

Click Stop, under the progress bar.

The photograph in hand is finished, so every file written is complete and checked; the rest are left for another time.

When It’s Finished

A summary says how many photographs were exported, any warnings, and any that couldn’t be, with the reason.

Projects

A project keeps your work: the queue, every photograph’s frames and screen settings, the output settings, and what was exported when. It doesn’t copy the photographs; it remembers where they are.

To Save Your Work

Choose Save from the File menu (or press Command-S), name the project, and click Save.

The window’s title becomes the project’s name. Save As saves a copy under another name.

If you close the window or quit with changes unsaved, MacPhoto Utility asks whether to save them.

To Open a Project

Choose Open Project from the File menu (or press Command-O), or choose one from Open Recent.

OR

Double-click the project in the Finder.

If Photographs Have Moved

MacPhoto Utility finds them again by their content, even moved to another folder or renamed, and says so. Save the project to remember the new places.

If a Photograph Is Missing

It stays in the list, marked Missing, with its frames kept.

Select it, and click Locate in the panel below the list; find the photograph and click Open.

OR

Click Locate in Folder to look in one folder for every missing photograph at once.

If the file you choose isn’t the same photograph (its content is different), MacPhoto Utility asks before using it.

To Start a New Project

Choose New Project from the File menu (or press Command-N).

The queue is emptied; the output settings stay as they were.

The Window

Everything happens in one window. On the left, the queue of photographs and the details of the one you’re working on; in the middle, the photograph with its frame, and the two previews; on the right, the output settings and the export buttons.

The window’s title is the project’s name, or Untitled. Closing the window quits MacPhoto Utility, asking first if there are unsaved changes.

Appendix B

The Files on the Macintosh Plus

The Macintosh Transfer Utility puts MacPhoto Utility’s files on a disk for the Plus, in the same three folders, and marks each one so the Plus knows what it is. CHECK

The Three Folders

Print FilesPrint images: PICT files in 256 greys, for MacPhoto. Type PICT, creator MPho.
PICT FilesScreen images: black-and-white PICT files, 512 × 342. Type PICT, creator MPho.
MacPaint FilesScreen images on MacPaint pages. Type PNTG, creator MPNT.

Files made by MacPhoto Utility open in MacPhoto when you double-click them (once MacPhoto exists). Until then, open them from inside a program.

Screen Images

Any program that opens PICT files shows a screen image: SuperPaint, for instance, or the Scrapbook. In an emulated Plus, SuperPaint 2.0 opened one and showed it exactly. CHECK

After Dark’s Slide Show

After Dark’s Slide Show shows the PICT files in the Slide Show Folder, in After Dark Files in the System Folder. Copy screen images there, and choose Slide Show in the After Dark control panel. CHECK

Keep print images out of it: the Plus’s own drawing can’t show pictures in 256 greys.

MacPaint

A MacPaint document holds the screen image in the middle of a MacPaint page: 32 dots in from the left and 189 down. MacPaint opens it like any of its own documents, and leaves your patterns as they were. In an emulated Plus it opened without trouble. CHECK

Print Images

A print image is for MacPhoto, the Plus’s photograph editor, which reads it a strip at a time, so it never needs the whole megabyte in memory. Photoshop 1.0 can open one, as a grey picture: it did in an emulated Plus. CHECK

Appendix C

Troubleshooting

If something doesn’t go as you expect, look for it here.

macOS won’t open MacPhoto Utility

It’s signed for its owner’s own use, not by Apple. Open System Settings, click Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway beside its name. You need do it only once.

A photograph won’t add

If nothing appears, it may not be a photograph MacPhoto Utility reads: click the “skipped” line under the list to see why. A photograph marked ✕ is damaged; its reason is shown when you select it (Appendix A).

A photograph in your Photos library can be added once it’s exported: in Photos, choose Export from the File menu.

A photograph is on its side

MacPhoto Utility turns photographs upright from the camera’s own record. If a photograph was saved without one, turn it with Command-[ or Command-].

The screen image looks too dark, or too flat

Drag Brightness or Contrast under the Mac Plus screen preview. Detail in deep shadow is often brought back by a little more brightness.

The print warns it’s small

Make the frame larger, or choose 300 PPI (Chapter 2, “Small Photographs”).

Export All skipped some photographs

Photographs that can’t be read are left out, and the summary says why. So are those you chose to skip because their files were already there.

A project’s photographs are missing

They’ve been deleted, or moved to another disk. Select one, and click Locate, or Locate in Folder to search a folder for all of them.

Nothing shows in After Dark’s Slide Show

Make sure the Slide Show Folder holds screen images (from PICT Files), not print images. CHECK

Glossary

Atkinson dithering

The way MacPhoto Utility turns greys into black and white dots, devised by Bill Atkinson for the first Macintosh: crisp, with clean whites and blacks.

brightness, contrast

Settings for the screen image only, from −100 to +100, under the Mac Plus screen preview.

click

To position the pointer on something, and press and quickly release the mouse or trackpad button.

crop

To leave part of a photograph out of a picture. Each photograph has a print crop and a screen crop.

dither

To show shades of grey with patterns of black and white dots.

export

To make the files: the print image, the screen image and, if chosen, a MacPaint document.

Finder

The program that shows your disks, folders and files, on your Mac and on the Plus.

frame

The rectangle drawn on the photograph, showing what a picture will hold.

HEIC

The kind of photograph an iPhone makes.

Macintosh Plus

The 1986 Macintosh the pictures are for, with a black and white screen 512 dots by 342.

Macintosh Transfer Utility

MacPhoto Utility’s companion, which puts files on a disk for the Plus.

MacPaint document

A picture MacPaint opens: 576 by 720 dots, black and white. MacPhoto Utility puts the screen image in the middle.

MacPhoto

The Plus’s photograph editor, which opens the print images.

manifest

The record, manifest.json, that MacPhoto Utility keeps in the export folder, of every file it made there.

missing

Of a photograph in a project: not where it was when the project was saved, and not found by its content.

opener

The picture across two pages at the start of each chapter.

PICT

The Macintosh’s picture file. The print image and the screen image are both PICT files.

pixel

One dot of a picture.

PPI

Pixels per inch: how many dots the print has to each inch. 360 or 300.

print crop

The frame that chooses what the print shows: 2.5 by 3.5 inches, portrait or landscape.

print image

The picture for printing and for MacPhoto: 2.5 by 3.5 inches, in 256 greys.

project

A file (.macphoto) that keeps the queue, every photograph’s frames and settings, and what was exported; never the photographs.

queue

The list of photographs on the left of the window.

relink

To tell a project where a missing photograph is now (Locate).

screen crop

The frame that chooses what the screen image shows: the shape of the Plus’s screen.

screen image

The picture for the Plus’s screen: 512 by 342 dots, black and white.

Slide Show

After Dark’s screen saver that shows the PICT files in its folder, one after another.

System 6

The Plus’s system software. MacPhoto Utility’s pictures are for System 6.0.7.

turn

To rotate a photograph a quarter at a time.

warning

A note under a preview that a frame is too small for its picture, which will be enlarged.

zoom

To make a frame smaller, so the picture shows less of the photograph, larger.

MacPhoto Utility

At a glance

  1. Add photographs (⌘I), or drag them in.
  2. Frame the print: drag, zoom, Portrait or Landscape.
  3. Frame the screen image (Tab), and set its brightness.
  4. Choose 360 PPI, the files and the folder.
  5. Export This Photograph (⌘E), or Export All (⇧⌘E).

Save (⌘S) keeps your work as a project. Screen images go in After Dark’s Slide Show Folder; print images never do.
